Wrought /rôt/
past tense of work. Shaped by hammering, not poured into a mould.

Cast iron is poured once and comes out brittle. Wrought iron is worked — heated, hammered, folded, over and over — and that is the only reason it bends instead of snapping. Nobody gets wrought in an afternoon. It is a record of what you actually did, which is exactly what this is.

One sentence a day.
It remembers the rest.

No forms. No weighing your rice in grams. No app to open and abandon by February. You say what you did the way you'd say it to a person, and it files it — food broken into macros, lifts into sets and loads, the scale into a trend rather than a number to panic about.

It lives where you already are

ChatGPT, Claude, or anything else that speaks MCP. Not another icon on your home screen — the assistant you already talk to every day, with a memory bolted on.

The verdict, every night

What happened, what you got right, what you got wrong with the number attached, and the one thing to do tomorrow. You choose how hard it hits: gentle, honest, or brutal.

Your watch fills in the rest

Sleep, steps, resting heart rate and weight arrive on their own overnight. Apple Watch works today through a free Shortcut. Rings and straps are next.

Progression you can actually see

Weight as a trend line, not a daily scare. Volume per session. A muscle-by-week matrix that shows the gap where legs used to be, without anyone having to say it.

Snacking is a time problem

Nobody eats 900 calories of crisps at 2pm. Set the hours you eat between and it counts down to them, instead of lecturing you at the fridge door.

It remembers the awkward parts

The knee that goes below parallel. The month you travel. The fact that you will never, ever go running. Said once, honoured forever.

Honest, not cruel.

The whole point is a thing that won't flatter you — so it names the 3,400-calorie day and the three weeks you skipped legs, with the numbers attached. But it is hard on the behaviour, never on you. It will not comment on your body. It will not shame you. And if the log starts looking like someone eating too little or dropping weight too fast, it stops coaching entirely and says so plainly — no framing, no workaround, no matter who asks.
