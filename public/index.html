Wrought

/rôt/

past tense of work. Shaped by hammering, not poured into a mould.

Cast iron is poured once and comes out brittle — drop it and it shatters. Wrought iron is worked: heated, struck, folded, heated again, until the grain runs the length of the bar. That is the only reason it bends instead of snapping, and there is no shortcut to it. Nobody gets wrought in an afternoon.

This is a record of work that was actually done. The name is the past tense of exactly that.

Your resting heart rate to the beat. Every run, every hour of sleep. All of it sitting on your wrist, never once mentioned to the thing you actually talk to. Wrought closes that gap — and it does it with one connection, not eight.

One thing said plainly: nothing on earth reads an Apple Watch continuously — Apple publishes no cloud API, at any price. Your phone pushes to us instead. Every app claiming a live Apple Health feed works this way; most just don't mention it.

The weight is never a guess. It comes from your own last session under a rule that only adds load when you hit the reps with something left in the tank — and it flatly refuses to invent a number for a lift you've never done. It asks you to find it instead. Beginner, intermediate or advanced changes not just the volume but how much gets explained.

Say "remember this as my leg day" and it's yours for good. Same for Tuesday five-a-side — a training log that can't hold football isn't a training log.

Both halves are estimates and it will never pretend otherwise. Resting burn varies by hundreds of calories between two people the same size, and a meal described in words is inferred, not measured. So it says roughly, and it tells you to correct it against the weekly trend on the scale — never against one day.

The part most apps won't do

Honest. Never cruel.

You can set how hard it hits — gentle, honest, or brutal — and it holds that line exactly. Nobody gets bluntness they didn't ask for, and nobody who asked for honesty gets a cheerleader. It will name the three-thousand-calorie Saturday with the number attached. It will not pretend a bad week was a solid effort.

But it is hard on the work, never on you. It doesn't comment on your body. There is no shame in it anywhere.

Where the honesty stops

If the log starts to look like someone eating far too little, or dropping weight dangerously fast, or training fourteen days without rest — the coaching stops. Not softens. Stops. It says so plainly and points you at a professional, and it will not suggest a bigger deficit under any framing, no matter who asks or how it's worded.

A blunt machine pointed at food and bodyweight is the one way a thing like this does real harm. That check runs on the server, before any AI gets a say.
